# Complete Deck & Fence

register: brand

## Product purpose

Marketing site for Complete Deck & Fence at completedeckandfence.com. It has one job: get a Middle Tennessee homeowner to request an estimate for a deck or a fence.

## Users

Homeowners in Middle Tennessee thinking about a new deck, a privacy fence, or both. Many arrive on a phone after seeing a yard sign or an ad. They want three answers fast: does this crew build what I need, do they work near me, and how do I get a price.

Scene: a homeowner standing in the driveway in bright afternoon sun, phone in hand, after spotting the sign down the street, deciding whether this company looks solid enough to contact. That scene is why the site is light, high contrast, and set in large type.

## Brand

Source materials supplied by the owner:

* Logo: a heavy red C with a black outline, the rest of COMPLETE in black extended heavy caps, and DECK & FENCE in wide tracked caps below.
* Yard sign: a white board with the logo and the line "Built for better backyards." set in an upright serif.
* Privacy fence ad: a black top block with "A little privacy. A lot more peace.", a fence photo, the line "PRIVACY FENCES • MIDDLE TENNESSEE", and a red "Request an estimate" button.
* Website concept: "Make outside feel complete.", "Custom decks. Beautiful fences. Better weekends.", "Built around your life.", "Plan your project", and "Get an estimate".

Color comes straight from the sign: paper white, sign black, signal red. Red is the brand's voice, so it is used with intent: the C, the calls to action, and the estimate section.

## Tone

Plain, confident, neighborly. Short sentences. Sound like the crew lead on a site visit, not like an agency. Concrete nouns (posts, gates, stairs, slope) beat adjectives.

## Owner preferences

* Keep the current fonts: Archivo, Instrument Sans, and Newsreader. The owner finds them easy to read.
* Keep the current colors. The owner is happy with them, so design passes should not redo the palette.
* No dashes of any kind in visible copy.
* No AI slop.

## Anti references

* Generic AI landing page patterns: a small label above every heading, scrolling keyword strips, grain overlays, frosted glass panels, gradient text, buttons that chase the cursor, and sections that fade up on scroll.
* Copy that sounds machine written: lists of three everywhere, lyrical metaphors, hype words.
* Invented proof: fake reviews, fake stats, fake years in business, fake badges.
* Rustic contractor clichés: wood grain textures, forest green and tan palettes, hand drawn fonts.

## Principles

1. Never state a fact about the business that the owner has not confirmed.
2. Phone first, and readable in direct sun.
3. Every section points toward the estimate form.
4. Each photo appears once. Concept images stay labeled until real project photos replace them.

## Unconfirmed, needs the owner

* Phone number, email, and where estimate requests should go.
* Exact services, including repairs, composite decking, picket fences, and drive gates.
* Service area towns.
* Whether estimates are free.
* The steps that follow when someone reaches out.
* License, insurance, years in business, and reviews that can be shown.
* Real project photos.
